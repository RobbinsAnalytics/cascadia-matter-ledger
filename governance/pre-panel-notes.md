# Pre-panel notes — what the author already believes is wrong

*Written 2026-08-28, BEFORE the Rule 7.4 panel was spawned. This exists so
N — the novel share of panel defects — can be measured rather than
reconstructed. Reconstructing it after the fact produces a flattering number
and disarms the retirement trigger.*

**Authorship, stated plainly.** The charts were built by a Claude Code session
against Aaron Robbins' specification; Aaron is the module owner and has already
given two rounds of feedback, both dispositioned in `chart-review.md` (findings
F-1 to F-18). This list is what remains suspected AFTER those rounds. Anything
below that the panel also finds is **not** novel.

## Suspected, chart by chart

**Chart 1 — the decomposition**
1. The annotation sits two rows above the bar it explains. It is clear of every
   mark, which is what K3 required, but the link to step 4 is now proximity-weak
   and a reader may not connect them.
2. Five of the nine bars are sub-day and render as near-invisible ticks. A
   reader may read them as missing, or as zero, rather than as real and tiny.
3. "The obvious query" and "The governed answer" are anchors, not rules, but
   they are drawn identically to the seven rule bars. A reader may count nine
   rules, or may not see where the seven begin and end.
4. The bars mix two kinds of quantity — two absolute answers and seven deltas —
   on one axis labelled "days". Nothing on the canvas says which is which.

**Chart 2 — the trend trap**
5. The y-axis is truncated at 125 days. The subtitle declares it, but a reader
   skimming will not read the subtitle.
6. Nothing on the canvas says which line is the trustworthy one. Dashed reads as
   "provisional" by convention, which happens to be right here, but by luck.
7. The whole point — that the two lines agreeing is an accident — is carried by
   one annotation. If that annotation is not read, the chart argues the opposite
   of its own thesis.

**Chart 3 — procedural progress**
8. "no court action" appears twice, distinguished only by the parenthetical.
   The fix made it uniform; it may still not make it obvious.

**Chart 4 — time to termination**
9. Category size is on every bar, but nothing says why it is there. A reader may
   not infer that a median over 5,032 and one over 588,952 are different claims.

**Chart 5 — disposition mix**
10. Two bars read "other", separated only by a code number. A reader has no way
    to know what code 14 and code 17 mean.
11. Twenty-one categories, of which the bottom eight are at or below 0.3%. Aaron
    already called this "chatter"; it was kept deliberately and may still read
    as clutter.

**Across all five**
12. No chart says what "contract matters" covers. The slice is one nature-of-suit
    family; a reader may take it for all civil litigation.
13. The subject-matter exclusion — carrier parties removed — is invisible on
    every chart.
14. At phone width the tooltips are gone by design, and no chart says the values
    are in the table below.
